    Democratizing Epistemic Access: Institutional Repositories, Diamond Open Access, and the Scholarly Commons

    Dr. Julian Thorne, Ph.D.
    Senior Academic Policy & Governance Advisor
    Published: July 01, 2026 Reading Time: 16 min read

    The Absurdity of the Commercial Academic Publishing Monopoly

    Consider the financial and operational mechanics of the commercial academic publishing industry, an enterprise that boasts profit margins exceeding 35% to 40%—eclipsing the margins of global tech titans and energy conglomerates. The research published in these commercial journals is conceptualized, executed, and analyzed by university scholars whose salaries and laboratory facilities are overwhelmingly subsidized by public tax dollars and university endowments. The manuscripts are written by those scholars for zero compensation. The peer review is conducted by other university faculty for zero compensation. The editorial boards are staffed by distinguished professors for negligible stipends.

    Yet, once this taxpayer-funded, scholar-created knowledge is completed, the commercial publishing houses demand that authors surrender their copyright. The publisher then locks the manuscript behind an exorbitant digital paywall, charging the very same university libraries that funded the research tens of millions of dollars in annual 'Big Deal' subscription bundles just to allow their faculty and students to read their own colleagues' discoveries. For researchers in the Global South, independent community scholars, medical practitioners, and the curious public, a single forty-page PDF article carries an absurd paywall toll of $45 to $60.

    This predatory business model represents one of the most brazen intellectual expropriations in modern history. The enclosure of the scholarly commons severely throttles scientific progress, blocks knowledge diffusion, and exacerbates global educational inequality.

    The False Promise of Commercial 'Gold' Open Access and APC Inequities

    In response to widespread international rebellion from university library consortia—manifested in landmark cancellations of commercial subscription bundles across Europe and North America—commercial publishers pivoted to a new monetization model: commercial 'Gold' Open Access financed through Article Processing Charges (APCs).

    Under the APC model, the paywall is removed from the reader and shifted onto the author. While anyone in the world can freely read an open access article, the author's institution or research grant must pay the publisher an exorbitant fee—often ranging from $3,500 to over $11,000 per paper—merely to have the manuscript published.

    Rather than democratizing knowledge, the APC regime has inverted the paywall into an 'author-wall.' It has created an insidious two-tier caste system in global scholarship: researchers at wealthy, elite institutions with massive grant budgets can easily afford to publish their findings in prestigious high-visibility journals, while brilliant researchers at underfunded regional universities, community colleges, and developing nations are economically disenfranchised from contributing to the global literature. The Gold APC paradigm has merely privatized a different end of the academic pipe, perpetuating systemic inequity under the disingenuous banner of 'open access.'

    The Diamond Open Access Solution: Institutional Infrastructure as a Public Good

    The authentic solution to the scholarly publishing crisis is Diamond Open Access (also known as Platinum Open Access). In a Diamond OA ecosystem, publications are completely free for readers to access and completely free for authors to publish. There are zero subscription paywalls and zero author-facing Article Processing Charges.

    How is Diamond OA financially and technically sustainable? By returning the stewardship of scholarly publishing to its rightful home: academic libraries, university presses, and scholarly societies. The true hosting and operational costs of modern digital publishing are a tiny fraction of what commercial oligarchs claim. When automated typesetting, open-source editorial workflow software (such as Open Journal Systems - OJS), and persistent digital archiving (via LOCKSS/CLOCKSS and Zenodo) are deployed, the actual marginal cost of publishing a rigorously peer-reviewed paper is less than $150 to $250.

    If global research universities diverted just 15% of the hundreds of millions of dollars currently squandered on commercial subscription paywalls into collaborative, library-funded Diamond OA consortiums, the entire world's academic output could be made perpetually open to every human on earth without a single author paying a dime.

    The AcademyVision Open Scholarly Commons: Architecture and Institutional Mandates

    At AcademyVision, we have translated the Diamond Open Access philosophy into immediate institutional policy through the AcademyVision Open Scholarly Commons (AV-OSC). Our comprehensive open knowledge infrastructure is grounded in four statutory pillars:

    1. Institutional Rights-Retention Policy: In accordance with the Harvard Open Access Policy model, all faculty members, doctoral fellows, and research staff grant AcademyVision a non-exclusive, irrevocable license to archive the author's accepted manuscript (AAM) of all scholarly articles in our institutional repository immediately upon acceptance, free of any publisher embargo periods. Faculty retain full copyright over their intellectual property.

    2. Endowed Diamond Publishing Presses: AcademyVision operates eight fully endowed, peer-reviewed academic journals spanning quantum physics, cognitive pedagogy, digital humanities, and environmental jurisprudence. All eight journals are operated on 100% Diamond OA standards, financed entirely through the university's research endowment.

    3. Interoperable Federated Repositories: The AV-OSC repository is built upon open-source DSpace and Fedora architectures, ensuring seamless OAI-PMH harvestability by global scholarly indexes, Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, and the Directory of Open Access Repositories (OpenDOAR). Every monograph is assigned a persistent DOI, schema.org academic metadata, and machine-readable Creative Commons licensing (CC-BY 4.0).

    4. Long-Term Preservation Architecture: In collaboration with the Internet Archive and decentralized IPFS academic nodes, all monographs archived within AV-OSC are mirrored across four geographically separated sovereign digital vaults, guaranteeing perpetual preservation regardless of geopolitical or climatic disruptions.

    Epistemic Justice and the Future of Shared Human Knowledge

    Knowledge is fundamentally different from material commodities. A loaf of bread or a barrel of oil is consumed when used; an idea, a mathematical proof, or a medical discovery multiplies and enriches the world the more widely it is shared, critiqued, and built upon. When Isaac Newton famously reflected, 'If I have seen further it is by standing on the shoulders of Giants,' he recognized that all human discovery is a communal, cumulative heritage.

    Locking the fruits of human intellect behind corporate paywalls is an assault on human flourishing and scientific advancement. By establishing vibrant institutional repositories, defending rights retention, and funding Diamond Open Access commons, universities fulfill their sacred mission: to serve as sanctuaries of truth and beacons of enlightenment for the whole of humanity.

    AcademyVision calls upon research institutions worldwide to dismantle the paywalls, reclaim our scholarly heritage, and open the gates of knowledge to all. When every student in rural Kenya, every independent scholar in Mumbai, and every citizen in Ohio can freely read the latest medical and climatic research, humanity's collective intellect will be fully unleashed.
